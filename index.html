⚠ Reference only

      
This estimator is for reference only and should not be used as the final tool to determine legality.

      

        How are these numbers calculated?
        
The calculation works backward from your maximum duty-off time, which is your report time plus the operational limit for your crew complement (CBA 12.D.1 — 20:00 for a four-person crew, 22:00 Hostile/ULR, 17:00 for three, and 11:30–15:00 for two by report time).

        
From maximum duty-off, subtract 30 minutes of post-flight duty to get your latest block-in, then subtract 10 minutes of taxi-in to get your latest touchdown. Subtract the OFP flight time from that touchdown to get your LATT (Latest Allowable Takeoff Time). Finally, subtract the OFP taxi-out time from the LATT to get your LABT (Latest Allowable Block-out Time).

        
This logic was verified step for step against the worked example in the IAP 2750 News Wire, Issue 221 (January 17, 2025), under “Legal to Start, Legal to Finish: Not at This Airline.”

        Open News Wire #221 ↗
      

      

        What do I do if I can't complete the flight within duty limits?
        
Coordinate with company ground personnel to inform them that the crew has run out of duty day to complete the flight. They should begin the process of coordinating with operations in GCC. Follow up with scheduling and inform them that your duty day will exceed the operational limit given the circumstances.

        
If a crew scheduler or a duty pilot attempts to convince you otherwise, kindly inform them that your limits are set by the CBA, which you wish to exercise. Additionally, you can reach out to the union Crew Desk to connect you to a steward.

        
Union Crew Desk: (717) 727-2750 · Option 2, Option 1.

        Who must be catered (Art. 5.E)
        
Catering is provided for all operating and deadheading Crew Members, and for jumpseaters who listed at least 12 hours in advance. The Company has no obligation to cater commercial deadheads or ground transportation legs.

        
Meals should endeavor to be American/Western style unless otherwise approved by the Union Catering Committee.

        
Choices: a minimum of two (2) meal choices when catering is required; a minimum of three (3) for augmented crews or when deadheading Crew Members are travelling.

        
Rotation: sandwiches and meal choices rotate approximately every two weeks.

        B737 hot meal alternative (MOU)
        
The B737 has no onboard ovens, so the Company is structurally unable to provide hot meals. On any B737 flight with scheduled block time of four (4) or more hours where a Crew Member would otherwise be entitled to a hot meal, they instead receive a gross payment of US $75.00 per meal.

        
The payment appears on your paycheck and is fully grossed up by the Company to cover associated taxes.

      

      

        Inoperative oven — Arbitration Award #AAI-2025-183
        
If an oven is inoperative on a flight where hot meals are contractually required, there is no automatic contract relief. The Company must remedy in this strict priority order:

        
    
          
  1. Provision a large meal-sized salad or sandwich (or breakfast equivalent) — from a catering vendor first, then a local restaurant.
    2. 
          
  2. Provision a pre-cooked hot meal from a catering vendor.
    3. 
          
  3. If mutually agreeable, authorize the pilot to purchase a meal on the Company credit card (or direct reimbursement).
    4. 
          
  4. Provision a hot meal from a local restaurant.
    5. 
          
  5. Provide a $100.00 meal voucher.
    6. 
        

        
Pilot's option: a pilot may unilaterally elect to bypass the priority list and take a $50.00 meal voucher in lieu of the remedies above.

      

      

        EHU (Ezhou) local catering procedures
        
EHU has only one local caterer, serving non-compliant meals, so alternative procedures were established:

        
Through catering: where aircraft space, ground time and combined block times allow, catering is loaded at the prior station and labeled by leg.

        
Hotel carry-on catering: when through-catering is impossible, place a food order with the front desk of your primary hotel (Wanda Hotel Huangshi or Crowne Plaza Ezhou) at least 16 hours prior to departure. The hotel prepares meals cold in oven-safe tins; the crew member carries them to the aircraft to reheat and charges the cost to their card/room (pre-authorized by accounting).

      

      

        Safety & enforcement
        
Unsecured galley items: all catering, including water cases and coolers, must be properly stowed and secured in galley cabinets and bins prior to departure. Loose items on the galley or flight deck floor are a severe safety hazard and violate FAA regulations and FOM policy.

        
No obligation to depart: operating crews, and Captains in particular, are under no obligation to depart until contractually compliant, safe and sanitary catering is on board.

        
Voucher refusal: outside the inoperative-oven award, the union strongly advises never accepting or negotiating a voucher in lieu of contractually required food. Captains cannot unilaterally accept vouchers on behalf of their crew.

        What do these terms mean?
        
Release check — a schedule check due 30 minutes after you block in, as you're released from the duty you just flew.

        
Minimum Rest Period — the contractual rest that begins right after you block in. You have no contactability during it.

        
Layover Period — your free time between the end of Minimum Rest and the start of Pre-Duty Rest. Ad-Hoc/Secondary/Reserve may owe 12-hr checks here.

        
Pre-Duty Rest Period — the 10-hour period right before your next report time. Your “10-hr pre-duty check” is due as it begins (report − 10:00).

        
32/168 — you must be given ≥ 32 consecutive hours free of all duty within any 168 hours before a duty period. A schedule check counts as duty, so some checks are waived to protect it.

      

    

    

    

      Authorized methods for a schedule check
      
A required schedule check must be completed via E-Crew, a mobile device, a personal computer, or by calling Crew Scheduling. Do not rely solely on the PCD (Personal Communications Device) — it may not show the most recent version of your schedule.
